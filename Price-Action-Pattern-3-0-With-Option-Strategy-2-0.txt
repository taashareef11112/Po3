In order to trade the double top pattern, it is important to understand how to enter
trades on both the breakdown and retest. Here's an example to demonstrate how to
enter trades and what to do when the market does not provide a retest.

Let's say we have identified a double top pattern on a stock. The first step is to wait
for a breakdown below the neckline of the pattern. Once the breakdown occurs, we
can enter a short trade with a stop loss above the highest point of the double top.

If the market provides a retest of the broken neckline, we can look for a retest entry.
This means waiting for the price to come back up to the neckline and then showing
signs of rejection or bearish confirmation. This could be in the form of a bearish
candlestick pattern, a lower high, or a decrease in volume. Once we have
confirmation of the retest rejection, we can enter a short trade with a stop loss above
the retest high.

However, there may be instances where the market does not provide a retest. In
such cases, we can still enter a short trade based on the breakdown signal alone.
This means entering the trade as soon as the price breaks below the neckline,
without waiting for a retest. In this scenario, we would still place a stop loss above
the highest point of the double top.

It's important to note that trading patterns like the double top requires careful
analysis, risk management, and consideration of other factors such as market
conditions and trend strength. It is recommended to practice and gain experience
before implementing such strategies in live trading.

In an uptrend market, things are completely opposite to a downtrend. In a
downtrend, we draw Fibonacci levels from top to bottom to find levels, while in an
uptrend, we draw Fibonacci levels from bottom to top to find levels. The psychology
behind this strategy is that buyers are able to make profits from the bottom, as the
market comes down to hit their stop loss and reduce their profits. When the market
starts moving back up after hitting their stop loss, we also enter a trade to capture
the upward movement.

The difference between a retest and a pullback is that in a retest, the market comes

In a pullback, the market does not come down after giving a breakout. It may form
one or two red candles and then continue with its trend.
